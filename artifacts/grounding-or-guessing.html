Fine-tuning didn't teach it to look. It taught it to guess more confidently.

Blur out a body region and a model that's actually reading the signer should get less sure of itself. We ran that test — one whole-frame pass, then a region-by-region pass over eyes, nose, mouth, face, shoulders and both hands — on the same 300 sentences, before and after LoRA fine-tuning on 4,000 How2Sign clips. Neither version reacts much to being blinded, and the fine-tuned one reacts less, mostly because it stopped describing the video at all.

The whole-frame test

Every clip, generated twice: once clean, once with the entire frame Gaussian-blurred. prob_drop is how much the model's confidence in its own words falls when blinded; hidden_shift is how much its internal state moves. A model reading the signer should show both, clearly above zero.

Zero-shot (pretrained) Fine-tuned (How2Sign LoRA)

Which region, if blurred, moves the needle

Same test, but masking one anatomical region at a time (a ±0.5s window around the cue frame), with a same-size random-patch control subtracted out so a region's size alone can't win. Bars are prob_drop — how much confidence falls when that one region goes dark. Ordered by how often linguists actually flagged each region as the real cue.

Zero-shot Fine-tuned

Internal disruption, region by region

hidden_shift — how much the model's last hidden state moves when that region is masked. Smaller across almost every region for the fine-tuned model: not just less confident when blinded, its internal computation barely notices. One caveat: masking face perturbs the internal state most for both models by a wide margin — largely a residual size effect the control correction doesn't fully remove, so treat cross-region ranking here as directional, not exact.

Model. Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, base vs. a LoRA adapter trained on 4,000 How2Sign clips (2 effective epochs before early stopping on rising val loss; see training run notes).
Method. Whole-frame pass: full Gaussian blur, teacher-forced on the model's own greedy prediction, confidence/hidden-state compared clean vs. blurred (297/300 clips scored; 3 failed decoding). Region pass: a ±0.5s window per sentence (frame count from that clip's own fps) around a CLIP-grounded cue frame, one of eyes / nose / mouth / face / shoulders / right hand / left hand blurred per pass using OpenPose landmarks, each score adjusted against a random same-size control patch (300/300 clips, same sentence–phrase pairs for both models).
Caveats. The region probe teacher-forces a single cue word after a single-word-answer prompt, not the full-sentence translation prompt used in fine-tuning — a proxy, not a like-for-like replay of either model's natural output. hidden_shift is more confounded by region size/salience than prob_drop; read the region-ranking chart as directional.

Scorers & methodology

Model

Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct — zero-shot (pretrained) vs. a LoRA adapter trained on 4,000 How2Sign clips (2 effective epochs before early stopping on rising val loss).

Whole-frame scorer

prob_drop
drop in the model's own-token confidence, clean vs. fully Gaussian-blurred frame, teacher-forced on its own greedy prediction.
hidden_shift
how far the model's last hidden state moves, clean vs. blurred.
nll_delta
negative log-likelihood increase when blurred.

297/300 clips scored (3 failed decoding).

Region scorer

Same prob_drop / hidden_shift pair, region-specific: a ±0.5s window (OpenPose landmarks) around a CLIP-grounded cue frame, one of eyes/nose/mouth/face/shoulders/left hand/right hand blurred per pass, each score corrected against a same-size random-patch control. 300/300 clips, identical sentence–phrase pairs for both models.

Text-overlap (secondary)

BLEU, chrF, ROUGE-L, Lexical F1 vs. ground truth — included for completeness; not treated as evidence of grounding (see write-up).
